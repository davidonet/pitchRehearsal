      What research says

      Why the app works the way it does, and where the evidence stops. The studies below are small and short, so read them as good reasons, not guarantees.

      Off-key singing is rarely tone deafness

      Congenital amusia, real tone deafness, was found in about 1.5% of people when tested on 20,850 participants. Older estimates put it near 4%.

      For most people who sing off-key, the likely weak link is turning a heard pitch into the right vocal movement. That is a motor skill, and motor skills respond to practice.

      Peretz and Vuvan, 2017, European Journal of Human Genetics [1]. Sensorimotor account: Musicae Scientiae, vol. 29 [3].
      0.0%
      1.5% current estimate, 3 in 200    older 4% estimate, 8 in 200

      Seeing your pitch helps more than hearing it alone

      Adults who sang off-key were randomly assigned to one 20-minute training. Everyone improved a little, but only the group that saw their pitch on screen, with a tone doubling their voice, improved significantly. The gain was largest on four-note melodies.

      A separate study found two real-time displays both improved accuracy, and a simpler display worked better for people with some singing background.

      Berglin et al., 2022, Psychology of Music [2]. University of Sydney study on real-time visual feedback [4].
      Each dot is one participant.

      Practise across a wide range

      Off-key singers tend to stay inside a narrow band of notes, more than their voice requires. In a single-session study, the group matching pitches across a full octave got better at matching single notes. The group limited to a fifth did not, and neither did a control group.

      "Effects of pitch range on singing accuracy training", Musicae Scientiae, vol. 29, pp. 240–255 [3].
      significant improvement    no significant change

      The evidence is short. Your plan is long.

      Each study above measured a single session. Six months of daily practice has not been tested in this way, so lasting gains are a reasonable bet, not a result.

      That is why the app scores your unaided notes separately: they are your own long-term evidence.

      Applies to [2] and [3]. Plan length assumes 20 minutes a day for 180 days.

      How Pitch Study uses this

      Guided mode shows and doubles your pitch

      The live mouth and gate are the visual feedback; the soft tone under your voice is the audio doubling from the strongest result.

      Exercises widen your range

      Targets expand beyond a comfortable band over time, following the octave-range finding.

      Unaided notes measure real progress

      Feedback fades as you improve so you don't depend on the screen. Fading is our design choice, not a tested finding.
